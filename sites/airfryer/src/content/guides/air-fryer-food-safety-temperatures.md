## Temperature, not time, tells you when food is safe

Every cook time you'll find here is a starting point, not a guarantee. Air fryers vary by wattage, basket size, and how full you pack them, so two machines can turn out very different results from the same number of minutes.

The only reliable way to know that meat, poultry, or fish is safe to eat is to measure its **internal temperature** with a food thermometer. Undercooked poultry, ground meat, and seafood can carry bacteria that make you sick—and you can't see, smell, or taste the difference. An inexpensive instant-read thermometer removes the guesswork.

### Why the timer can fool you

- Wattage and airflow differ from model to model.
- A crowded basket cooks slower than a single portion.
- Fridge-cold food takes longer than room-temperature food; frozen takes longer still.
- Thick cuts finish later than thin ones, even at the same weight.

## USDA safe minimum internal temperatures

Measure at the thickest part and compare against these targets from the USDA:

| Food | Safe minimum internal temperature |
| --- | --- |
| Poultry (chicken, turkey) | 165°F / 74°C |
| Ground meats (beef, pork, lamb) | 160°F / 71°C |
| Whole cuts of beef, pork, veal, lamb (steaks, chops, roasts) | 145°F / 63°C, then rest 3 minutes |
| Fish | 145°F / 63°C |
| Egg dishes | 160°F / 71°C |
| Leftovers and reheated food | 165°F / 74°C |

For whole cuts, that three-minute rest matters: the temperature keeps climbing after you pull the food and holds long enough to finish the job. Ground meats need a higher target than whole cuts because grinding spreads surface bacteria throughout the meat.

## Where to insert the thermometer

Slide the probe into the **thickest part** of the food, away from any bone, and don't let the tip poke through into the hot basket—both will give you a falsely high reading.

- **Chicken breast:** the center of the thickest end.
- **Chicken thighs or wings:** the meatiest spot, avoiding the bone.
- **Burgers and meatballs:** straight into the middle, through the side.
- **Salmon and other fish:** the thickest part of the fillet.

If you're cooking several pieces, check the largest one—and ideally a couple of others, since air fryers don't heat perfectly evenly.

## The foods to watch most closely

Poultry and ground meat deserve the most attention. Chicken should always reach 165°F/74°C, with clear juices and no pink at the joint as secondary signs. Burgers and other ground meats hide their risk on the inside, so a thermometer is the only honest check.

Whole cuts like steak and pork chops are more forgiving because bacteria stay on the surface, which the hot air sears quickly. Fish is done at 145°F/63°C, when it flakes easily with a fork.

When in doubt, put the thermometer in. It takes five seconds and settles the question completely.
