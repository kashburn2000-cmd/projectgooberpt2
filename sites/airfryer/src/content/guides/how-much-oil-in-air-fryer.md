## The honest answer: little to none

The whole point of an air fryer is that it crisps food with fast-moving hot air instead of a bath of oil. So you need far less than you would in a pan — often none at all.

How much depends entirely on what you're cooking.

## Oil or no oil? A quick guide by food type

- **Frozen breaded foods** (fries, nuggets, mozzarella sticks): **No oil.** They're already coated in enough — adding more just makes them greasy. Browse the [frozen foods](/category/frozen) for times.
- **Fresh vegetables** (broccoli, Brussels sprouts, potatoes): **A light toss.** About ½ to 1 teaspoon per basket helps them brown and keeps seasoning on. See the [vegetables](/category/vegetables) section.
- **Lean proteins** (chicken breast, shrimp, tofu): **A light spritz or brush** so the surface doesn't dry out.
- **Fatty meats** (bacon, sausage, chicken thighs): **No oil.** They render plenty of their own — adding more is what makes an air fryer smoke.

When in doubt, start dry. You can always add a spritz next time; you can't take it back out.

## Which oils to pick

Air fryers run hot — most cooking happens at **375–400°F** — so choose an oil that can take the heat without smoking. Good high-smoke-point choices:

- Avocado oil
- Refined (not extra-virgin) canola or vegetable oil
- Light or "refined" olive oil

Skip extra-virgin olive oil for high-heat cooking; its low smoke point means it can burn and turn bitter. You can match temperatures to foods on the [air fryer temperature chart](/air-fryer-temperature-chart).

## Skip the aerosol cooking spray

This one matters for your machine, not just your food.

**Don't use aerosol cooking sprays** — the pressurized cans of PAM-style spray. They contain additives and propellants that build up on the basket and **can damage the non-stick coating** over time, leaving a sticky, hazy residue that never fully cleans off.

Instead:

- Fill a **refillable pump mister** with your own oil, or
- **Brush** on a thin layer with a pastry brush or a folded paper towel.

Both give you control over exactly how much goes on — which, again, should be a little.

## A little goes a long way

Too much oil is one of the most common air fryer mistakes: it pools in the drawer, smokes on the hot element, and leaves food heavy instead of crisp. If yours is smoking, oil is a likely culprit — here's [why your air fryer smokes](/guides/why-is-my-air-fryer-smoking).
