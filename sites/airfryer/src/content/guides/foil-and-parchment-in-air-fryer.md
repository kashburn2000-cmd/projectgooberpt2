## The short answer: yes, with rules

You can use both aluminum foil and parchment paper in an air fryer. They make cleanup easier and keep sticky glazes off the basket. But an air fryer is not an oven — it moves a lot of hot air very fast, and anything light enough to move can end up somewhere dangerous.

Follow a few rules and you'll be fine.

## Never preheat with loose parchment or foil

This is the one that starts fires, so read it twice.

**Never run your air fryer with empty parchment or foil inside.** With no food weighing it down, the fan can lift a loose sheet straight up into the heating element, where it can scorch and catch fire in seconds.

Only add parchment or foil **with food already on top of it**, and add it right before you cook — not during a preheat.

## Do this

- Add parchment or foil **only when food is holding it down**.
- Use **perforated air-fryer parchment** (the pre-cut rounds with holes) so air still moves.
- Leave the edges clear so hot air can circulate around the food.
- Keep parchment **below its temperature rating** — check the box, usually around 400–425°F.
- Use foil to wrap messy foods or catch drips under fatty cuts.

## Don't do this

- **Don't** preheat with an empty sheet inside (see above).
- **Don't** cover the whole basket floor — blocking the holes wrecks the crisping that makes an air fryer worth using.
- **Don't** let foil touch the heating element.
- **Don't** use foil with very acidic foods like tomatoes, citrus, or vinegar marinades — the acid reacts with the metal, which can leave a metallic taste and pit the foil.

If airflow is your goal — and it usually is — see how a clear basket helps you [get food crispy in an air fryer](/guides/how-to-get-food-crispy-in-air-fryer).

## Foil vs. parchment at a glance

| Liner | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Parchment** | Sticky, saucy, or breaded foods | Must be perforated; keep under its temp rating |
| **Foil** | Wrapping, catching grease drips | Acidic foods; never near the element |
