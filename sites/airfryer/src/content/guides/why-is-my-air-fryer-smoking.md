## First, don't panic

A little smoke usually isn't a broken machine — it's grease meeting a hot heating element. Once you know what's dripping and burning, it's an easy fix.

Here are the usual causes, from most to least common.

## 1. Fat dripping onto the element

This is the big one. High-fat foods — **bacon, sausage, ground beef, well-marbled chicken thighs** — render hot grease as they cook. That grease drips down, hits the exposed heating element, and burns into smoke.

The fix that works best:

- **Add about a tablespoon of water to the bottom drawer** before cooking fatty foods. The water keeps drippings from scorching on the hot metal. (This trick is just for greasy foods — dry, low-fat foods don't need it.)

Cooking [bacon](/food/bacon) is the classic example — a little water underneath makes a real difference.

## 2. Leftover grease from the last cook

If you smell smoke before you've added anything fatty, the culprit is probably residue from your **previous batch** baked onto the basket or element.

- **Clean between batches** when you're cooking in rounds.
- Wipe the element down (unplugged and fully cool) if grease has built up on it.
- A full clean-up routine lives in [cleaning an air fryer](/guides/cleaning-an-air-fryer).

## 3. Too much oil

More oil doesn't mean crispier food — it means pooled oil that smokes. Use a light spritz or none at all; see [how much oil to use](/guides/how-much-oil-in-air-fryer).

And **never use aerosol cooking sprays.** Beyond the smoke, their propellants can damage your basket's non-stick coating. Use a pump mister or a brush instead.

## Quick troubleshooting checklist

Run through these in order when smoke appears:

1. **Is it a fatty food?** Add a tablespoon of water to the drawer.
2. **Did you just cook something greasy?** Stop and clean the basket and element.
3. **Did you add oil?** Use less, and never an aerosol can.
4. **Is food or foil touching the element?** Reposition so nothing hits the top.
5. **Still smoking with an empty, clean basket?** Unplug it and check with the manufacturer — that's the rare case that isn't grease.

## The short version

Nine times out of ten, air fryer smoke is burning grease — from the food, from last time, or from too much oil. Keep the basket clean, go easy on the oil, and float a little water under the fatty stuff. For more habits worth breaking, see [air fryer mistakes to avoid](/guides/air-fryer-mistakes-to-avoid).
