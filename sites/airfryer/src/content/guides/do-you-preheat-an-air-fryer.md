## The short answer

You don't have to preheat an air fryer every time, but a quick preheat genuinely helps when you want crisp, browned surfaces. For soft, bake-style foods it makes little difference and just costs you a few minutes.

An air fryer cooks by rapidly circulating hot air, much like a small convection oven. When the basket and heating element are already hot, food starts searing the moment it goes in instead of slowly warming up. That head start is what gives you crisp edges and even browning.

## What preheating actually does

- Kick-starts searing, so surfaces crisp instead of steaming
- Improves consistency, so your second batch cooks like the first
- Trims the cook time a little, since the food isn't heating the machine

Preheating doesn't make everything cook "better." Moist, delicate foods often turn out the same whether the basket started hot or cold.

## When to preheat — and when to skip

### Preheat for crisp, seared foods

Preheat when a browned, crunchy exterior is the whole point:

- Steaks, chops, chicken thighs, and burgers you want seared
- Fries and other frozen foods meant to be crunchy
- Reheating pizza, wings, or anything you want crisp again, not soggy

If crispiness is the goal, a preheat pairs well with the other habits in [how to get food crispy in an air fryer](/guides/how-to-get-food-crispy-in-air-fryer).

### Skip it for gentle, delicate foods

Skip the preheat when it adds little or could work against you:

- Bake-style items like cakes, egg bites, and casseroles that cook gently
- Delicate foods that can dry out or scorch, such as thin fish or pastry
- Long-cooking dishes where a couple of minutes won't change the outcome

## Preheat or skip: a quick list

| Preheat | Skip |
|---|---|
| Fries and frozen crispy foods | Cakes and baked goods |
| Steaks, chops, burgers | Egg bites and casseroles |
| Reheating for crispness | Delicate fish or pastry |
| Anything you want seared | Long, slow-cooking dishes |

## How to preheat

1. Set the air fryer to your cooking temperature — most foods land between 375–400°F.
2. Run it empty for 2–5 minutes. Smaller baskets need less; larger ones need a touch more.
3. Add the food and start cooking. Since the basket is already hot, check a minute or two early the first time.

If your model has a preheat button, use it; otherwise just run it at the cook temperature. You can confirm target temps on the [air fryer temperature chart](/air-fryer-temperature-chart).

## One thing preheating won't fix

A hot basket can't rescue a crowded one — pile food in and it steams no matter how well you preheated. And for any meat, browning is not the same as *done*. Always confirm with a thermometer, as covered in [air fryer food safety temperatures](/guides/air-fryer-food-safety-temperatures).
