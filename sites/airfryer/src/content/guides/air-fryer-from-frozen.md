## Skip the thawing—frozen goes straight in

One of the best things an air fryer does is cook frozen food beautifully, straight from the freezer. There's no thawing, no soggy microwave step, and no waiting around. The circulating hot air hits the food from every side, crisping the outside while the inside heats through.

For most freezer staples, you just tip them into the basket and start cooking. That's it.

## Why the air fryer loves frozen food

Frozen convenience foods—fries, nuggets, and the like—are usually par-cooked at the factory and coated to crisp up with heat. That's exactly what an air fryer delivers. Compared with an oven, it preheats fast and concentrates the heat, so you get a crunchier result in less time and without a tray of oil. ## How to adjust your timing

Frozen food starts colder than fresh, so it takes a little longer—but less than you'd expect. A few habits make it foolproof:

- **Add a few minutes** over the fresh cook time, then check early the first time you make something.
- **Don't overcrowd.** A single layer with space around each piece crisps far better than a heaped basket.
- **Shake or turn partway.** Frozen pieces often start clumped together, then loosen as they thaw. Shake the basket once they separate so every side gets airflow.
- **Skip added oil for coated foods.** They already have enough; a light mist only helps plain items like uncoated vegetables.

## A few favorites

These freezer staples are some of the most popular things to make in an air fryer, and they're forgiving enough that you'll dial them in after a batch or two.

- **[Frozen french fries](/food/frozen-french-fries):** shake once or twice, then pull them when the edges go deep golden.
- **[Frozen chicken nuggets](/food/frozen-chicken-nuggets):** turn them halfway; they crisp fast, so watch the end.
- **[Salmon](/food/salmon):** cooks well from frozen—just add time and check that it flakes.

## Frozen raw meat still needs a safe temperature

Here's the one rule that never bends. Par-cooked snacks only need to be hot and crisp, but **frozen raw meat, poultry, and fish must still reach the USDA safe internal temperature.** Cooking from frozen simply means allowing extra time to get there.

Use a food thermometer at the thickest part:

- Chicken and other poultry: **165°F / 74°C**
- Ground meats: **160°F / 71°C**
- Fish: **145°F / 63°C**

Time is only a guide; temperature is what makes food safe. Frozen pieces can hide cold spots in the center, so this check matters even more than it does with fresh food. If the thermometer reads low, give it a few more minutes and test again.
