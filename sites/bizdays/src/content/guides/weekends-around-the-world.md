## The weekend isn't universal

Most people treat "the weekend" as Saturday and Sunday. For most of the world, that's correct. But a large bloc of countries rests on different days, and if you set deadlines, schedule meetings, or ship across borders, those differences change when a working day actually lands.

A [business day](/guides/what-is-a-business-day) is a weekday that isn't a public holiday. The catch is that "weekday" depends on where you are. Move the weekend, and you move which days count.

## Why the weekend varies

Religious observance is the main driver. In many Muslim-majority countries, Friday is the day of congregational prayer, so rest days historically centered on Friday. In Israel, the Jewish Sabbath runs from Friday evening to Saturday evening, which pairs naturally with a Friday-Saturday weekend.

Global trade pulls the other way. As banking and commerce standardized on a Monday-to-Friday week, several countries shifted their weekends to overlap more with international markets. The result is a patchwork you can't assume away.

## Weekend patterns by country

| Weekend days | Countries (examples) |
| --- | --- |
| Saturday + Sunday | Most of the world: US, UK, most of Europe, China, Japan, Brazil, Australia, and the UAE (since 2022) |
| Friday + Saturday | Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Jordan, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Iraq, Israel |
| Friday only | Iran |
| Friday + Sunday | Brunei |
| Saturday only | Nepal (historically) |

### The UAE switch

In January 2022, the United Arab Emirates moved from a Friday-Saturday weekend to Saturday-Sunday, keeping a shortened workday on Friday. It was the first Gulf state to align its weekend with global markets. The rest of the Friday-Saturday bloc kept their traditional weekend, so don't assume the whole region changed with it.

## Why this matters for your deadlines

The friction shows up whenever two calendars overlap. A Monday-to-Friday office and a Sunday-to-Thursday office share only four common working days each week: Monday through Thursday. Friday is a workday for one and a rest day for the other, and Sunday flips it.

Keep these traps in mind:

- **Cross-border deadlines.** "Five business days" counted in Riyadh and counted in London won't land on the same date.
- **Response times.** A request sent Thursday to a Friday-Saturday country waits through their weekend, not yours.
- **Cutoffs and handoffs.** Payments, shipping, and approvals stall on the receiving side's rest days, not the sending side's.
