## The short answer

Most months have between **20 and 23 working days** — weekdays that aren't public holidays. A common rule of thumb is around 21 working days per month, which lines up with roughly 250 working days spread across 12 months. But the exact figure shifts from month to month and country to country.

## Why the number varies

Three things move the count:

- **How long the month is.** A 28-day February holds exactly four weeks, or 20 weekdays. A 31-day month can fit up to 23 weekdays, depending on where the weekends land.
- **Which day the month starts on.** A month that begins on a Saturday "spends" its first two days on the weekend, often leaving fewer weekdays than one that starts on a Monday.
- **Public holidays.** Each holiday that falls on a weekday knocks one day off the total. A month with two such holidays can drop from 22 working days to 20.

Because of that last point, the same calendar month has a different working-day count in different countries. December in the United States and December in the United Kingdom don't match, because their [public holidays](/holidays) differ.

## Weekdays before holidays

Before you subtract any holidays, here's how the raw weekday count falls out by month length:

| Month length | Minimum weekdays | Maximum weekdays |
|--------------|------------------|------------------|
| 28 days (Feb) | 20 | 20 |
| 30 days | 20 | 22 |
| 31 days | 21 | 23 |

Then subtract the public holidays that fall on a weekday for your country. A 31-day month with 23 weekdays and one weekday holiday leaves you 22 working days.

## How to count a specific month

### Do it by hand

To get the exact figure for, say, March 2026 in a given country:

1. Count the weekdays (Monday–Friday) in the month.
2. List that country's public holidays for the month.
3. Subtract each holiday that falls on a weekday — a holiday already on a weekend doesn't reduce the count.
