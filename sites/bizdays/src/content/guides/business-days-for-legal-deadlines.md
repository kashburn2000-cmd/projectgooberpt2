> **This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Deadline rules differ by country, state, and even by individual court. The counting method that applies to you may not match the general patterns below. Always confirm the exact rule with the relevant statute, court rule, or a qualified lawyer before you rely on any date.

## Business days vs. calendar days

The first question with any deadline is which kind of day it counts:

- **Calendar days** count every day — weekends and public holidays included. "Within 30 calendar days" means 30 days straight, no skipping.
- **Business days** (sometimes called court days or working days) count only weekdays that are not public holidays.

The same number produces very different dates depending on which basis applies. "10 business days" can stretch across two full weeks or more once weekends and a holiday are removed.

## The "clear days" concept

Many systems count in **clear days**, which exclude *both* the first and the last day of the period. In practice that usually means you:

- exclude the day the clock starts (for example, the day a document is served or filed),
- exclude the day the deadline itself lands, and
- count only the full days in between.

Other systems include the first day, the last day, or neither. Because the convention is not universal, a "5-day" deadline can resolve to different dates under different rules.

## How weekends and holidays factor in

When a period is counted in business or court days, weekends and public holidays are skipped as you count. Two more patterns show up often:

- **Rollover.** If a deadline lands on a weekend or holiday, many rules push it forward to the next working day. Some instead move it backward, and a few do not move it at all.
- **The right holiday list.** Business-day counting depends on the correct jurisdiction's holidays. A court may recognize local or regional closures that a generic national list misses.

## An illustrative comparison

This example is illustrative only — it shows the *shape* of the difference, not a rule you can rely on.

| Basis | "10 days" from a Monday | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calendar days | Second Thursday | Counts weekends and holidays |
| Business days | About two weeks later | Skips 2 weekends; more if a holiday falls inside |
| Clear days | Starts the day *after* the trigger | First and last day excluded |

## Before you rely on a date

Use this as a checklist, not a conclusion.

### Confirm before you count

- Identify the jurisdiction and the specific court or statute that sets the deadline.
- Confirm whether it counts calendar days, business days, or clear days.
- Confirm which holiday list applies and whether a rollover rule shifts the final date.
- Use a tool like the [business-day calculator](/) or the [public holidays directory](/holidays) to visualize the window — then have the actual date confirmed by the court rule or a lawyer.
