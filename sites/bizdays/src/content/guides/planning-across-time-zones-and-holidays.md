## Why cross-border schedules slip

When two teams sit in different countries, they rarely share the same working calendar. One side's normal Tuesday can be the other side's national holiday, and one side's weekend may not even fall on the same days. A deadline that looks like "five working days" to you can be shorter — or longer — for the person on the other end.

The fix is to plan against **both** calendars at once, not just your own.

## Find the overlapping working days

A day only counts as productive for a project when it is a working day for everyone who has to act on it. Start by mapping each side's weekend and holidays, then keep the days both sides share.

- Confirm each country's weekend. Saturday and Sunday is common, but not universal — see [weekends around the world](/guides/weekends-around-the-world).
- List each country's public holidays for the period from the [holidays directory](/holidays).
- Keep only the days that are working days on *both* calendars.

| Day | Country A | Country B | Shared? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fri | Working | Weekend | No |
| Sat | Weekend | Weekend | No |
| Mon | Holiday | Working | No |
| Tue | Working | Working | Yes |

The shared days are the ones you can actually schedule against.

## The holiday you'll forget

The most common cause of a missed handoff is a holiday on the *other* side that you never see on your own calendar. Long national breaks — think multi-day holiday weeks — can freeze an entire counterpart team for days while your side keeps working and assumes progress is happening.

Before you commit to a date, look up the other country specifically. Use the [countries directory](/countries) to find it, then check its holidays for your project window.

## Build in buffers

Even with a shared calendar mapped, give yourself room:

- Add a buffer of one to three working days around any cross-border handoff.
- Avoid setting a hard deadline on the last shared working day before a long holiday on either side.
- Account for the time-zone gap itself — if the overlap in the workday is only a few hours, a single round trip of questions can eat a whole day.
- Confirm *whose* working days the deadline is counted in, and count them with the [business-days tools](/business-days).

## A two-country scheduling checklist

Run through these before you lock a date.

### The checklist

- [ ] Identified both countries' weekend days.
- [ ] Pulled both countries' public holidays for the full project window.
- [ ] Marked the working days the two calendars share.
- [ ] Checked for long holiday breaks on the other side.
- [ ] Added a buffer before any holiday-adjacent handoff.
- [ ] Agreed which side's calendar the deadline is measured against.
