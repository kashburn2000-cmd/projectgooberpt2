You see the phrase everywhere — "ships in 2 business days," "net 30 business days,"
"allow 5–7 business days for your refund." But what *is* a business day, exactly?

## The definition

A **business day** (also called a *working day*) is any day that is:

- **not a weekend**, and
- **not a public holiday** in the relevant country.

That's it. In most of the world a business day is Monday through Friday, minus the
public holidays that fall on those weekdays. Our [calculator](/) applies both rules
automatically for whichever country you pick.

## Why "Monday to Friday" isn't universal

The weekend is a cultural convention, not a law of nature. While Saturday–Sunday is
the most common weekend, it's far from the only one:

| Weekend | Where (examples) |
|---|---|
| Saturday & Sunday | Most of the Americas, Europe, East Asia, Australia |
| Friday & Saturday | Much of the Middle East & North Africa — Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Jordan, Israel |
| Friday only | Iran |

So "3 business days" starting on a Wednesday means something different in New York
than it does in Riyadh. If you schedule across borders, this is exactly the kind of
detail that quietly breaks deadlines — see
[weekends around the world](/guides/weekends-around-the-world).

## Holidays are the other half

Even a normal Tuesday isn't a business day if it's a public holiday. And holidays
are country-specific: the [United States](/holidays/united-states) closes for
Thanksgiving; the [United Kingdom](/holidays/united-kingdom) has bank holidays the
US has never heard of. A calculator that only skips weekends will overcount your
working days every time a holiday lands mid-range.

## Business day vs. calendar day

The counterpart to a business day is a **calendar day** — every day, weekends and
holidays included. The two are used in different contexts, and mixing them up is a
common, costly mistake:

- **Business days:** shipping estimates, service-level agreements, processing times.
- **Calendar days:** most payment terms (net 30), notice periods, subscription renewals.

More on that in [business days vs. calendar days](/guides/business-days-vs-calendar-days).

## The takeaway

A business day = a weekday that isn't a public holiday, *for a specific country*.
Get the country's weekend and holiday list right and the rest is just counting —
which is what this site does for you.
