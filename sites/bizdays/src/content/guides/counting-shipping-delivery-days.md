## Business days aren't calendar days

When a checkout page promises "3-5 business days," it is not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or public holidays. It usually isn't counting the day you ordered, either. That gap between a business-day estimate and the [calendar days](/guides/business-days-vs-calendar-days) you actually wait is why a package can feel late when it's right on schedule.

Three rules cover almost every case:

- **Weekends don't count.** In most countries that means Saturday and Sunday.
- **Public holidays don't count.** A holiday inside the window pushes delivery a day later.
- **The order day usually doesn't count.** Counting typically starts the next business day.

## Carrier cutoff times matter

Every carrier and warehouse has a daily cutoff. Order before it, and today can start the clock; order after it, and your order is treated as if it came in the next business day.

Say the cutoff is 2:00 p.m. An order placed at 1:00 p.m. Monday begins processing Monday. The same order at 4:00 p.m. is handled as Tuesday's. That one-hour difference can move your delivery date by a full day, or more if a weekend follows.

## A worked example: order Thursday, "2 business days"

Assume you order Thursday before the cutoff, with a standard Saturday-Sunday weekend and no holidays.

| Calendar day | Status | Business-day count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thursday | Order placed (before cutoff) | Day 0 — not counted |
| Friday | Weekday | Day 1 |
| Saturday | Weekend | Skipped |
| Sunday | Weekend | Skipped |
| Monday | Weekday | Day 2 — delivered |

Two business days becomes a four-day wait on the calendar. Nothing went wrong; the weekend simply doesn't count.

### Add a holiday

Now suppose that Monday is a public holiday. Day 2 slides to Tuesday, and your two-business-day order lands five calendar days after you placed it. This is why the same "2 business days" promise can produce different arrival dates depending on the week. You can check which dates are holidays on the [holidays](/holidays) page before you count.

## How to estimate your own arrival date

1. Start from the first business day *after* your order clears the cutoff.
2. Count forward only on weekdays.
3. Skip any public holiday you hit.
4. Land on the last counted day for the earliest likely delivery.

For a range like "3-5 business days," run the count twice: once for the low number and once for the high number. That gives you the honest earliest-to-latest window. To skip the manual counting, drop your order date into the [calculator](/) and let it handle the weekends and holidays for you.
