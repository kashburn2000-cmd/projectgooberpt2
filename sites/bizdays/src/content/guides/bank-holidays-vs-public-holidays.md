## Same idea, different labels

"Public holiday," "bank holiday," and "federal holiday" get used as if they mean the same thing. They overlap, but they aren't identical, and the differences decide what's actually open on the day.

**Public holiday** is the general, worldwide term for a day officially set aside for rest or observance. The other two are regional names with more specific legal weight.

## Bank holidays: the UK and Ireland

In the United Kingdom and Ireland, statutory days off are called [bank holidays](/holidays/united-kingdom). The name is literal: it began with legislation that closed the banks, and because commerce ran through the banks, most other businesses and schools closed too.

A few things surprise people:

- A bank holiday is not an automatic right to paid time off. Whether you get the day, or pay for working it, depends on your contract.
- The list isn't uniform across the UK. Scotland, England and Wales, and Northern Ireland each have their own set.
- In everyday speech, "bank holiday" and "public holiday" are used interchangeably there, even though the strict term is "bank holiday."

## Federal holidays: the United States

In the United States, a [federal holiday](/holidays/united-states) strictly binds only the federal government. On these days:

- Federal offices and the Federal Reserve close, so most banks and the mail service pause.
- States set their own holidays and are not required to match the federal list.
- Private employers decide for themselves whether to close or pay, unless a state law says otherwise.

So a US federal holiday is a guarantee that Washington and the banking system stop, not that every business does.

## Quick comparison

| Term | Where it's used | Primarily closes | Legal reach |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public holiday | Worldwide (umbrella term) | Varies by country | General concept |
| Bank holiday | UK and Ireland | Banks, and most businesses and schools by custom | Set by statute |
| Federal holiday | United States | Federal offices, USPS, Federal Reserve banks | Binds the federal government only |

## Regional and local holidays stack on top

National holidays are rarely the whole story. Regions add their own days on top of the national list: a US state may observe a holiday the rest of the country ignores, and a UK nation may keep a day its neighbors don't. Cities and provinces elsewhere do the same.

For counting purposes, this is what matters most. When you work out a [business day](/guides/what-is-a-business-day), the label on the holiday doesn't change the math, but *which* days count as holidays does, and that depends on the exact country and region.
