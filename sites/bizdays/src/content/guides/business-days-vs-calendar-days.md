## Two ways to count "days"

Not all days are counted the same way. When someone promises something in "10 days," the answer depends entirely on which kind of day they mean.

- A **calendar day** is any day on the calendar — every Monday through Sunday, holidays included.
- A **business day** (or working day) is a weekday that isn't a public holiday. For most countries that means Monday through Friday, minus national holidays. For the full definition, see [what is a business day](/guides/what-is-a-business-day).

The gap between the two is the weekends and holidays you skip. Over a few days it's small; over a month or a quarter it adds up fast.

## Quick comparison

| | Calendar days | Business days |
|---|---|---|
| Counts weekends? | Yes | No |
| Counts public holidays? | Yes | No |
| Days in a typical week | 7 | 5 |
| Typical use | Payment terms, notice periods, interest | Shipping estimates, SLAs, processing times |
| End date predictable? | Always the same | Depends on the country's holidays |

## Where each one shows up

### When you'll see business days

You'll usually see business days when the promise depends on people being at work:

- Shipping and delivery estimates ("arrives in 3–5 business days")
- Service-level agreements (SLAs) and support response times
- Bank and payment processing ("funds clear in 2 business days")
- Refund and return windows

### When you'll see calendar days

You'll usually see calendar days when the count is contractual or financial:

- Payment terms like net 30 — see [net 30/60/90 payment terms](/guides/net-30-60-90-payment-terms)
- Notice periods for cancellation or termination
- Interest, late fees, and statutory deadlines

The safest rule of thumb: if a document just says "days," assume calendar days unless it specifically says "business" or "working" days.

## The same "10 days," two different dates

Suppose today is **Friday, March 6, 2026**, and there are no holidays in the window.

- **10 calendar days** later: count every day, including both weekends. You land on **Monday, March 16, 2026**.
- **10 business days** later: skip both Saturdays and both Sundays. You land on **Friday, March 20, 2026**.

Same starting point, same "10 days," four days apart. Add a public holiday inside the window and the business-day date slides even later, while the calendar-day date doesn't move at all.
