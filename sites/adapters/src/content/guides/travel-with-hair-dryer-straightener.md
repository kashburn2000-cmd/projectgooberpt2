Hair dryers, flat irons, and curling wands are the **number-one devices travelers
destroy abroad** — and it's almost always the same story. They're often
single-voltage, and they pull a lot of power. Get it wrong and you don't get a
second chance; you get a pop, a puff of smoke, and a dead tool.

## Why styling tools are different

Your phone charger is dual-voltage and barely sips power. A hair dryer is the
opposite: it's a **heating appliance**, and heat needs serious wattage — often
1,200 to 1,875 W. Two things follow from that:

- Many styling tools sold for one market are **single-voltage** (120 V in North
  America, 230 V across much of the world). Plug a 120 V dryer into a 230 V socket
  with just a plug adapter and you can destroy it instantly.
- Their high wattage makes them very hard to run through a converter — more on that
  trap below.

Not sure whether yours is single- or dual-voltage? Read the label; the whole
approach is explained in
[do I need a voltage converter?](/guides/do-i-need-a-voltage-converter).

## The cheap-converter trap

The instinct is to buy a small "travel converter" and call it solved. For a hair
dryer, this usually **doesn't work.** Those inexpensive converters are typically
rated for a fraction of what a heating appliance draws, and many are meant only for
short bursts. Ask one to feed a 1,500 W dryer continuously and you'll trip it,
overheat it, or burn out your tool. A converter that can genuinely handle that load
is a heavy transformer that costs and weighs more than a new dryer.

## The two options that actually work

### Option 1: buy a dual-voltage travel model

The clean fix. Look for a tool with a **120/240 V switch** or a label reading
**"100–240V."** These adjust to either voltage band on their own — no converter,
ever. Travel-specific dryers are also lighter and fold down small. This is the
right call if you genuinely need your own tool (thick hair, a specific styler,
professional use).

**One catch:** dual-voltage handles the *electricity*, not the *plug shape.* You
still need the correct [plug adapter](/plug-types) for your destination's sockets.

### Option 2: use the hotel's hair dryer

The easiest fix of all — leave yours at home. Most mid-range and up hotels provide
an in-room dryer already wired for local voltage. It won't be a salon-grade tool,
but for drying hair on a trip it's usually plenty, and it's one less thing to pack,
worry about, or fry.

## Quick decision list

1. **Dual-voltage (100–240V or a voltage switch)?** Pack it, plus the right plug
   adapter. Done.
2. **Single-voltage, and your destination is the same band?** Fine — just add a
   plug adapter if the sockets differ.
3. **Single-voltage, different band?** Don't force it through a cheap converter.
   Buy a dual-voltage travel model, or use the hotel's.
4. **Only need it occasionally?** Skip it entirely and use what the hotel provides.
