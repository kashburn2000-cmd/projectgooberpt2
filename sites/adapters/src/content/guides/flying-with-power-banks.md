Power banks are one of the few travel gadgets with real rules attached — and the
rules are stricter than most people expect. The good news is they're simple once
you know the two things that matter: **where the battery rides** and **how big it
is.** Here's everything you need before you fly.

## Why power banks have special rules

Lithium-ion batteries can overheat and, in rare cases, catch fire. In the cabin, a
crew member can deal with a smoking battery in seconds. In the cargo hold, no one
would even know until it became a serious problem. That single fact drives every
rule below.

The authorities to know are the FAA's **PackSafe** program
(faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe) in the United States and **IATA's dangerous-goods
guidance**, which most airlines worldwide follow. They agree on the essentials.

## Rule one: carry-on only

Power banks and any spare or loose lithium batteries must travel in your
**carry-on bag — never in checked luggage.** This is not a suggestion; it's a firm
rule at essentially every airline. If your bag gets gate-checked on a full flight,
pull your power bank and spare batteries out and keep them with you in the cabin.

## Rule two: watt-hour limits

How big a battery you can bring is measured in **watt-hours (Wh)**, not
milliamp-hours. There are three tiers:

| Capacity | Allowed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Up to 100 Wh** | Yes — no approval needed | Covers almost every phone and laptop power bank |
| **100–160 Wh** | Yes — but only with airline approval | Usually limited to 2 spare batteries per passenger |
| **Over 160 Wh** | No | Not allowed in the cabin or checked baggage (only certain approved equipment) |

The reassuring part: a typical phone power bank sits comfortably in the first tier.
You almost certainly have nothing to worry about.

## How to calculate your power bank's Wh

Many power banks print their Wh rating right on the case — look for a number like
"74Wh." If yours only lists milliamp-hours (mAh), the math is quick:

**Wh = (mAh ÷ 1000) × voltage**

Lithium-ion cells have a nominal voltage of about **3.6–3.7 V.** So for a common
20,000 mAh bank:

> (20,000 ÷ 1000) × 3.7 = **74 Wh**

That's well under the 100 Wh limit, so it flies with no approval needed. A bank
around **27,000 mAh** lands near the 100 Wh line — so if you're eyeing a
huge-capacity model, run this calculation *before* you buy.

## Packing them safely

- Keep each battery in its **original packaging**, or **tape over the terminals**,
  to prevent short circuits.
- Carry spare batteries **individually** — don't let loose cells rattle together
  against keys or coins.
- Keep power banks somewhere you can reach, not buried in a bag you might have to
  gate-check.

## Before you fly

Rules can vary by **airline and country**, and the figures above are the widely
used baseline — not a guarantee for every carrier. Always confirm the specifics
with your airline, especially for anything over 100 Wh. A two-minute check of their
website beats being turned away at security.
