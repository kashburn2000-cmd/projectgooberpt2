Europe looks like a patchwork of plugs, but for a traveler it's simpler than it
appears. Across almost the whole continent the electricity is identical — 230V at
50Hz — and most countries share compatible sockets. So can one adapter cover the
entire trip? Very nearly.

## The good news: one voltage, mostly one plug

Continental [Europe](/region/europe) runs uniformly on 230V / 50Hz. Voltage is
never the variable here: if your gear is dual-voltage — and nearly every phone,
laptop, and camera charger is — you'll never need a converter in Europe, only the
right plug shape.

Most of the mainland uses two closely related plugs:

- **Type C** — the two-pin Europlug, which physically fits sockets across the
  continent.
- **Type F (Schuko)** — the grounded standard in Germany and much of central
  Europe.

These two are cross-compatible: a Type C plug drops straight into a Schuko socket.
So a single Type C / Schuko adapter handles the vast majority of the mainland —
Germany, France, Spain, Portugal, the Netherlands, Austria, Scandinavia, Poland,
and most of Eastern Europe. (France's sockets are technically Type E, but they take
the same plugs.)

## The exceptions worth packing for

A handful of places break the pattern. If your route includes any of these, add
the right adapter:

| Country / area | Plug type | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| UK, Ireland, Malta, Cyprus, Gibraltar | Type G | A completely different three-pin plug — always pack a G adapter |
| Switzerland, Liechtenstein | Type J | A slim Type C plug fits, but a bulky Schuko will not |
| Italy | Type L | Many Italian sockets also accept Type C; some newer ones take Schuko |
| Denmark | Type K | Danish sockets accept Type C; a Schuko may not fully seat |

### Two patterns to remember

Two patterns fall out of that table. The [United Kingdom](/country/united-kingdom)
and Ireland — plus Malta, Cyprus, and Gibraltar — use Type G and simply need their
own adapter. And [Switzerland](/country/switzerland), Italy, and Denmark each take
a slim Type C Europlug, so a thin ungrounded adapter often gets you by — but none
reliably accepts a chunky Schuko, and a bare Type C plug carries no ground.

## So, one adapter or several?

- **For a single mainland country** (say Germany or France): one Type C / Schuko
  adapter is all you need.
- **For a multi-country mainland trip:** that same adapter covers you almost
  everywhere.
- **Add a Type G adapter** if the UK or Ireland is on the itinerary.
- **For Switzerland, Italy, or Denmark:** a slim Type C adapter usually works;
  bring a country-specific one if you need a guaranteed ground for a three-pin
  appliance.

For most travelers the honest answer is: buy one good multi-country adapter — many
combine C, G, and a grounded option in a single unit — and you're set. Not sure
what your exact route needs? [Check your countries](/) and we'll spell it out plug
by plug.
