Charging your phone, tablet, and other USB gear in another country is the easiest
part of travel power — once you understand one fact. Get that fact, and you can
leave a drawer full of chargers at home.

## The one fact that makes USB charging easy

Anything that charges over a USB or USB-C cable is inherently dual-voltage. The
charger — the block that plugs into the wall — does the real work: it takes
whatever the mains delivers, anywhere from 100V to 240V, and steps it down to the
low-voltage DC your device actually uses. Check the small print on almost any USB
charger and you'll see `INPUT: 100–240V, 50/60Hz`. That range covers the whole
world.

So your phone, tablet, e-reader, headphones, smartwatch, power bank, and camera
don't care what country they're in. The only thing that changes from place to
place is the shape of the wall socket. You never need a bulky voltage converter
for USB gear — just a plug adapter that fits the socket. (If that distinction is
new to you, read [adapter vs. converter](/guides/adapter-vs-converter) first.)

## Pack one good charger, not five

Because every USB charger accepts the same worldwide voltage, you can ditch the
pile of individual bricks. The smart move is a single multi-port USB / GaN charger
plus one plug adapter:

- **GaN (gallium nitride) chargers** run cooler and pack more power into less
  space, so a 3- or 4-port unit is barely bigger than a matchbox.
- One charger with a couple of USB-C and USB-A ports can top up your phone,
  tablet, watch, and headphones overnight from a single wall socket.
- You carry one plug adapter instead of several — less to lose, less to forget.

### USB-C Power Delivery can run your laptop too

Modern USB-C Power Delivery (PD) has quietly changed what "USB charging" means. A
65W or 100W USB-C PD charger can power not just phones and tablets but many recent
laptops — anything that charges over USB-C. If your laptop uses a USB-C charger,
one good PD charger may genuinely be the only power supply you need for the whole
trip. Always confirm your laptop's own brick reads `100–240V` (nearly all do). For
a device-by-device rundown, see [will my devices work abroad?](/guides/will-my-devices-work-abroad)

## Don't cheap out on the charger

The charger is the one part of this setup handling live mains voltage, so it's the
one part worth spending on. A reputable, properly certified charger has the
insulation and protection circuitry that keeps mains voltage safely away from your
devices — and from you. Ultra-cheap, no-name chargers are exactly where those
safeguards get skipped.

- Buy from a known brand and look for genuine certification marks.
- Match the wattage to your needs (65W or more if you want to charge a laptop).
- Retire anything with a cracked case, frayed cable, or scorch marks.

## Packing checklist

- One multi-port USB / GaN charger — add USB-C PD if you travel with a laptop.
- A [plug adapter](/plug-types) for your destination's socket shape.
- Your usual charging cables, plus one spare (cables fail more often than chargers).
- A small power bank for long travel days.
- No voltage converter — USB gear never needs one.
